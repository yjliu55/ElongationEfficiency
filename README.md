Codes used in the paper "An intron-position-weighted method for quantifying relative Pol II elongation efficiency in Arabidopsis"

RNA polymerase II (Pol II) elongation shapes gene expression and co-transcriptional RNA processing but remains poorly characterized in plants, partly because robust and scalable approaches for measuring transcriptional kinetics are lacking. Here, we establish Pol II Elongation Efficiency (PEE), a position-weighted framework that quantifies relative Pol II progression from intronic pNET-seq profiles. We validate its robustness across biological replicates, experimental protocols, and Pol II signals, and systematically determine how sequence context, co-transcriptional splicing, chromatin organization, and intron length affect its interpretation. Using PEE, we show that Pol II progression changes during leaf aging and in response to temperature, salt, and immune-related cues. These changes are generally modest, occur predominantly at the gene level, and are largely uncoupled from transcriptional output. By enabling reliable quantification of transcriptional elongation from nascent RNA data, PEE provides a practical framework for studying transcriptional dynamics in plants and for investigating how developmental and environmental signals regulate the transcription cycle.
